What format should the ads be?

Format determines the entire creative brief — copy length, visual structure, and production requirements.

A

Static Image — Andrew's Face

Photo of Andrew + headline overlay + short copy

Personal brand ads consistently outperform generic stock imagery for financial services. Andrew's story (grew up without protection, now helps families) is a differentiator. Face = trust signal. Easiest to produce — works with the photos already in brand_assets.

Production effort: Low — use existing photos
Expected CPL: Lowest — personal ads convert well for independent agents

B

Static Image — Lifestyle / Family

Family/home imagery + strong headline + Engel Financial Group branding

Safe, professional, brand-focused. Works well but is harder to differentiate from every other insurance ad. No personal connection. Better if Andrew doesn't want to be the face of ads yet.

Production effort: Low — stock or AI-generated imagery
Expected CPL: Medium — blends in with competitors

C

Short-Form Video — Andrew Talking to Camera

30–60 sec UGC-style video: Andrew speaks directly, no fancy production

Highest trust-building format. "I grew up without this — here's why I do what I do" is a powerful hook. UGC-style (no studio, natural lighting, direct eye contact) currently performs best on Meta for financial services. Requires Andrew to record a few clips.

Production effort: Medium — Andrew records on phone, no studio needed
Expected CPL: Lowest overall — video builds connection fast

D

Mixed — Static Now, Video Later

Launch with static images (fast), layer in video once campaign is live

Pragmatic approach: get data flowing now with static ads, then introduce video creatives once you have baseline CPL to compare against. Doesn't delay launch.

Production effort: Low to start, medium when video is added
Expected CPL: Starts medium, improves when video is added
